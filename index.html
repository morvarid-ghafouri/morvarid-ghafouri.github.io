About

Research that reaches the patient.

I'm a biomedical engineer specializing in EEG signal processing and brain-computer interfaces, with one goal: building technology that gives people with disabilities more independence.

As Cofounder and Head of R&D at Cafe IMED, I lead the development of BCI systems and work closely with clinicians to move rehabilitation technology from the lab into real medical settings. My research centers on translating motor-imagery EEG into control commands, most notably a four-way EEG-and-joystick-controlled wheelchair, now a national patent.

I've also worked on electrical hand prostheses and physiotherapy robotics, and in 2025 I wrote two chapters for edited volumes on neuroengineering and next-generation BCI architectures. Years of implementing ISO 13485 standards for medical device organizations keep my research grounded in what can actually be built and certified.

Research & Projects

From EEG cap to working device

Selected projects across brain-computer interfaces, prosthetics and rehabilitation robotics.

M.Sc. thesis · National patent 2025

EEG-controlled smart wheelchair

A four-way wheelchair driven by the user's motor-imagery EEG and controllable alongside a joystick. The pipeline covers preprocessing, signal decomposition, feature selection and classification, benchmarked on BCI Competition IV dataset 2a and then run online on custom control hardware.
